I just watched David Heinemeier Hansson talk to Lex Fridman for five hours.
Five. Hours. And the whole time I kept thinking: this guy is just like me.

Then I did the math.

Where I'm a third-level wizard, DHH is an eleventh-level wizard. Where he can
hold sixteen threads in his head and run sixteen agents at once, I top out at
three before I start dropping things. Where he has an unlimited bucket of tokens
to throw at a problem, I'm watching the meter — every bit of agentic magic I
cast costs me actual dollars, and I feel each one.

Same spellbook. Very different mana pool.

But here's the thing about Omarchy — the Arch-and-Hyprland rig DHH built, and
then spent a good chunk of those five hours enthusing about — it closes the gap
more than it has any right to.

## The keyboard made me honest

First, it forced me onto the keyboard. Properly. No more hunting for a trackpad,
no more digging through menus. `Super` + a letter and the window goes where I
want it. It felt hostile for about a day and then it felt like my hands had been
lying to me for twenty years.

## The actual magic

But the keyboard isn't the headline. The headline is that there is now a direct
line from me to an AI agent to my operating system *and my hardware*.

An AI-first OS. Game changer, and I don't throw that phrase around. I don't
think I understood what it meant until I lived in it for a week.

Small example. I dock my laptop to a USB-C hub, lid down, and I wanted it to
stay awake and then wake when I wiggle the mouse. I could have figured that out
— `logind` config, `hypridle`, some incantation about lid switches — an hour of
open tabs, probably. Instead I typed one sentence to the AI and it was so. Done.
Correct. Explained back to me.

That is not a party trick. That is the friction of owning a computer just...
dissolving.

## The app thing is genuinely bonkers

And then there is what you build on top of it. Off the top of my head, lately:

- A **Lode Runner** plugin, because why should a game not live in my window
manager
- A replacement for **Typora** — my own Markdown editor, and honestly it suits
how I work better than the thing it replaced. I'm writing this in it.
- A **speedometer** app for my phone

None of these took a weekend. They took an evening, or an afternoon, or a prompt
and a coffee.

## So what is this going to be like in a year?

That's the question I can't put down.

Right now I'm a third-level wizard casting spells that would have been a whole
dev team five years ago, on an OS that treats "just ask it" as a first-class
input method. The tools get better every month. My token budget is the main
thing holding me back, and even that curve points the right way.

Give it a year. The eleventh-level wizards will be doing things I can't picture
yet. But the gap to *them* was never the point. The point is where *I'll* be —
still third level, sure, but a third-level wizard with Omarchy and another
year's practice is going to be dangerous.

I'm agog. That's the only honest word for it.
